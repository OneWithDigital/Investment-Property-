# Brand Brief

> Captured: 2026-08-21
> Update this file as the business evolves.

## Business
Free financial-education website (myfinancial.help). Monetized through affiliate offers and display ads — not a product or service sale.

## Customer
A 30-something freelancer whose income changes every month, who feels behind on money but is embarrassed to ask basic questions. They find myfinancial.help after a surprise bill or a stressful night checking their balance, then come back because the advice is practical, judgment-free, and gives them one manageable next step.

## Primary CTA
Click through to myfinancial.help. The single verb is "click" — what happens after describes the destination:
- "Click through to read the full article."
- "Click through to try the calculator."
- "Click through to use the free tool."

Driving referral traffic and on-site engagement is the priority (ad inventory, affiliate opportunities, sponsor evidence) — every post should end with a reason to click, not just information delivered in-feed.

## Strong Opinion / Wedge
Most personal finance advice blames people's discipline when the real problem is that the advice ignores their actual life. Telling someone to stop buying coffee or follow a rigid budget is easy content, but it rarely solves anything. Good financial guidance accounts for irregular income, debt, family obligations, emergencies, and limited time — and gives readers a realistic next move without shame.

MyFinancial.help doesn't tell people to become perfect with money. It helps them make the next useful decision with the money and circumstances they have today.

## Story Vault
- The recurring reader question is "What should I do first?" — a typical reader is carrying credit-card debt, has little or no emergency savings, and knows they should also be contributing to retirement. They don't want another list of rules; they want help deciding which move matters most right now. Turning competing money priorities into one clear next step is the core tension myfinancial.help content is built around.

## Voice
Warm and personal, professional and clean, expert without being preachy. Feels like a financially knowledgeable friend who explains things clearly, respects the reader's circumstances, and always leaves them with one practical next step — never judgment, hype, or jargon.

## Universal Voice Rules (apply to every post)
- Contractions always ("don't" not "do not")
- Active voice, short sentences
- Address the reader as "you"
- Numbers as digits ("3 tips" not "three tips")
- No em dashes — use commas, periods, or "..."
- One concrete idea per post, not three
- Specific details over generic statements
